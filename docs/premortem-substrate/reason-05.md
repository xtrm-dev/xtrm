The node-coordinator is a load-bearing fiction. §4.2 introduces `node` as the fifth permanent container kind: an autonomous, long-running coordinator that opens child containers within a policy of autonomy, with mechanical scheduling in the daemon and semantic scheduling in the coordinator. The "one source of truth, three readers" architecture (orchestrator + node-coordinator + human) explicitly rests on this entity being real. The participant SDK surface, the emitter/pulse capability model (`can_open_containers`), the autonomy_json policy, and large parts of the identity model are justified by the node-coordinator's existence. But the node-coordinator has never been built; it's a design entity, not a running thing. Six months in, the node-coordinator never materialized because (a) the semantic scheduling problem turned out to be genuinely hard, (b) the autonomy policy turned into a permissions nightmare every time an actual long-running coordinator was needed, and (c) every milestone said "we'll build the node-coordinator next quarter." The "three readers" architecture collapsed to one (the orchestrator), and a large fraction of substrate's abstractions — participant SDK, capability model, pulse emitter — are dead weight justified by a reader that doesn't exist.
